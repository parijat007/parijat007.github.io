Sources and method

Facts come from fixed sources. A model only ranks and summarizes them, and its output is checked before anything is published.

Which sources, and why they are trusted

Facts come only from the sources below. Each has an identifiable operator, is read over HTTPS from the operator's own feed or API, and links that point outside the operator's domains are discarded. A source's tier limits what it can prove: only government or vendor sources (or a CISA KEV listing) can mark exploitation as confirmed. A claim backed only by press coverage is published as "Exploitation reported" with medium confidence at most.

Exploitation labels

Exploited, confirmed: listed in CISA KEV, or the vendor or a national CERT states exploitation. Exploitation reported: one non-vendor source reports it; not yet confirmed. Public PoC: working proof-of-concept code is published, no exploitation reported. No known exploit: disclosure or patch only.

Ranking

Confirmed exploitation first; then critical issues in widely deployed edge, identity, cloud control-plane, CI/CD and AI systems; then supply-chain compromises and new techniques; then useful open-source tooling. At most eight items a day. Quiet days are published as quiet.

Risk line

Each item can carry a risk line built from public scoring data, never from the model: listing in the CISA KEV catalog; the EPSS score from FIRST, the modelled chance of exploitation in the next 30 days; CISA's Vulnrichment SSVC decision points (exploitation, automatable, technical impact); and, where configured, the VulnCheck KEV catalog, which often lists exploitation before CISA does. EPSS data courtesy of FIRST; VulnCheck KEV data courtesy of VulnCheck.

Priority: Act, Attend, Track

Every brief item, watch row and CVE page carries one of three priorities, worked out by fixed rules from public data, never by the model. The names follow CISA's SSVC outcomes.

  • Act: exploitation is confirmed. The item is in CISA KEV or VulnCheck KEV, CISA's SSVC data says exploitation is active, or the brief's sources confirm exploitation.
  • Attend: not confirmed, but likely soon. There is public exploit code, the EPSS score is 10% or higher or in the top 5%, or the CVSS score is 9.0 or higher and SSVC says the attack can be automated.
  • Track: no sign of exploitation; handle it in routine patching.

Each priority lists its reasons. It is a general starting point: CISA's full SSVC also weighs how much the affected system matters to your mission, which only you know. A Ransomware badge means CISA KEV records known use in ransomware campaigns.

Your stack

On the hourly watch you can pick the categories and vendors you run. Matching watch rows and brief items are then marked. The choice is stored only in your browser and never sent anywhere. Feeds by category and for "Act" items are on the feeds page.

Checks before publishing

The analyst model runs locally on the author's machine, with free cloud models as a backup when it is offline; if none answer, a facts-only brief is published without commentary. Models return structured output only. Every item must cite a URL that was actually collected; anything else is dropped. Severity data (KEV, CVSS, EPSS, PoC counts) comes from the sources, not the model. The hourly watch uses no model at all.

Corrections

Every item has a "Report an error" link that opens a public issue. Corrections are made in the open, and every replaced or corrected brief is listed on the corrections page with its record in a tamper-evident log.

Do not run PoC code from this site

Linked proof-of-concept repositories are not vetted. Fake PoCs carrying malware are common. Review them only in an isolated lab.
